Betty

photo: Betty the day she was parked, summer 2025

Betty is a 1993 Range Rover Classic LWB with the 4.2 V8. She was the daily driver until summer 2025, when she was parked to do the jobs you can't do on your only car, head gaskets included. Standing did what standing does.

This is the story of bringing her back, in three Parts.

Part 1: Recommission

In progress · Sept 2026 to now · 4 entries

photo: ignition contact block opened on the bench

Getting her running and driving safely. First job: the key does nothing. So far the trail runs through a flat battery, a battery lead corroded through at the crimp, and an ignition contact block that shorts to its own housing.

Part 1: Recommission

Running and driving safely. In progress.

Summer 2025: parked

Last day as a daily driver. The head gaskets were already due.

September 2026: a flat battery and fresh petrol

photo: battery on the charger

Moved once while parked, then found completely flat. Recharged, fresh petrol in. Build Log entry

September 2026: the key does nothing

No dash lights, no fuel pump prime, no crank. Jumping 12 V onto an ignition output wakes the dash, so the fault is at or before the ignition switch. Case: No start: key does nothing

September 2026: the contact block

photo: contacts coated in yellowed grease

Out, opened, cleaned. It still shorts the battery feed to its alloy housing at II and III, three reassemblies running. A new block is the sensible call. Build Log entry

What's next

  • New Lucas contact block
  • Remake the battery positive lead
  • Turn the engine by hand before the first crank
